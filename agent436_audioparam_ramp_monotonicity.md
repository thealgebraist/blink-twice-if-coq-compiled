**AUDIT REPORT: BLINK WEBAUDIO SUBSYSTEM**
**Agent ID:** 436
**Target:** `blink::AudioParamTimeline` (AudioParam Automation & Ramping)
**Subject:** Mathematical & Computational Monotonicity of Parameter Ramps

---

### 1. Executive Summary
According to the W3C Web Audio API Specification, automation ramps (`linearRampToValueAtTime` and `exponentialRampToValueAtTime`) transitions between two scheduled events must be strictly monotonic (or monotonically non-decreasing/non-increasing if start and end values are equal). 

An audit of Blink’s `AudioParamTimeline` implementation (specifically within `third_party/blink/renderer/modules/webaudio/audio_param_timeline.cc`) reveals that while the **ideal mathematical formulas are strictly monotonic**, the **computational implementation is vulnerable to non-monotonic artifacts** due to IEEE 754 single-precision (`float`) arithmetic, SIMD vectorization boundaries, and recurrence relation drift.

---

### 2. Theoretical Monotonicity (Spec Definition)
Let $t_0, t_1$ be the start and end times of a ramp ($t_0 < t_1$), and $v_0, v_1$ be the start and end values. Let the normalized time factor be $\alpha(t) = \frac{t - t_0}{t_1 - t_0}$ for $t \in [t_0, t_1]$. Since $t$ advances strictly by the sample period $\Delta t = 1/F_s$, $\alpha(t)$ is strictly increasing.

1. **Linear Ramp:** 
   $$v(t) = v_0 + (v_1 - v_0)\alpha(t)$$
   The first derivative with respect to $t$ is $\frac{v_1 - v_0}{t_1 - t_0}$, which has a constant sign. **Strictly monotonic.**

2. **Exponential Ramp:** 
   $$v(t) = v_0 \left(\frac{v_1}{v_0}\right)^{\alpha(t)}$$
   Valid only when $\text{sgn}(v_0) = \text{sgn}(v_1)$ and $v_0, v_1 \neq 0$. The derivative preserves its sign across the entire interval. **Strictly monotonic.**

### 3. Blink Implementation & Monotonicity Vulnerabilities

Blink processes audio in render quanta of 128 frames (`kRenderQuantumFrames`). Ramps are computed across arrays of `float` (32-bit IEEE 754) values, while timeline time (`double`) is tracked in 64-bit precision.

#### A. Linear Ramp Audit (`kLinearRampToValue`)
**Mechanism:**
Blink calculates the step increment $\Delta v = (v_1 - v_0) \cdot \frac{\Delta t}{t_1 - t_0}$ and often utilizes SIMD (`vector_math`) or an unrolled loop to compute $v[k] = v_0 + k \cdot \Delta v$.

**Monotonicity Vectors:**
1. **SIMD vs. Scalar Boundary Discrepancy:**
   In vectorized implementations, a vector of 4 or 8 floats is initialized as $[v_0, v_0+\Delta v, v_0+2\Delta v, v_0+3\Delta v]$, and subsequent vectors are generated by adding $4\Delta v$ repeatedly. Because floating-point addition is not associative, $(v_0 + 3\Delta v) + 4\Delta v$ may differ in the Last Place (ULP) from scalar fallback loops used for the tail end of a ramp (when the ramp ends mid-quantum). In edge cases with extremely shallow slopes, this transition from SIMD to scalar calculation can cause a 1-ULP reversal, violating strict monotonicity.
2. **Endpoint Snap Discontinuity (Overshoot):**
   Accumulating $\Delta v$ over millions of samples (e.g., a 60-second ramp) results in floating-point drift. Just before $t_1$, the accumulated value $v[n-1]$ may slightly exceed $v_1$ (for an upward ramp). At $t \ge t_1$, the timeline snaps the value to the exact target $v_1$. This snap creates a microscopic downward step at the end of an upward ramp, breaking monotonicity.

#### B. Exponential Ramp Audit (`kExponentialRampToValue`)
**Mechanism:**
Computing `std::pow` per sample is prohibitively expensive for the real-time audio thread. Blink optimizes this using a first-order recurrence relation. It calculates a multiplier:
$$m = \left(\frac{v_1}{v_0}\right)^{\frac{1}{\text{num\_sample\_frames}}}$$
And iterates per sample: `value *= multiplier`.

**Monotonicity Vectors:**
1. **Catastrophic Cumulative Drift (The Snap Reversal):**
   Because `value` and `multiplier` are often truncated to `float` for buffer writing, repeated multiplication compounds rounding errors rapidly ($O(N)$ error growth). Over long ramps, `value` will almost certainly drift past $v_1$ (overshoot) or fall short (undershoot). If it overshoots, the transition to the post-ramp intrinsic value ($v_1$) results in a sharp non-monotonic step reversal at $t_1$.
2. **Subnormal (Denormal) Flushing:**
   When ramping exponentially toward a very small number (e.g., $10^{-38}$), values may enter the subnormal range. Blink sets the FTZ (Flush-to-Zero) and DAZ (Denormals-Are-Zero) CPU flags on the audio thread to prevent performance degradation. This causes the ramp to abruptly drop to `0.0f` before reaching $t_1$. While technically still monotonic (non-increasing), it violates the *strict* monotonicity of an exponential curve and breaks the invariant that exponential ramps cannot reach zero.
3. **Zero/Sign-Change Bypass:**
   If $v_0$ and $v_1$ have opposite signs or either is zero, mathematical monotonicity of the exponential function breaks down (becomes complex or undefined). Blink handles this by degenerating the event into a step function (`SetValueAtTime` at $t_1$). This maintains weak monotonicity (flat, then a single step).

### 4. Other Timeline Events (Context)

* **`setTargetAtTime` (`kSetTarget`):** 
  Implemented via a 1-pole IIR filter: $v[n] += (v_{\text{target}} - v[n]) \cdot c$, where $c = 1 - e^{-\Delta t / \tau}$. 
  *Audit:* Strictly monotonic until $v[n]$ approaches $v_{\text{target}}$ closely enough that $(v_{\text{target}} - v[n]) \cdot c$ underflows the precision of `v[n]`'s mantissa, causing the value to stall permanently a few ULPs away from the target (limit cycle stall). It remains monotonically non-decreasing/increasing, never oscillating.
* **`setValueCurveAtTime` (`kSetValueCurve`):**
  Linearly interpolates between user-provided `Float32Array` points. Monotonicity is only guaranteed *between* adjacent array indices (subject to the same linear interpolation float limits noted in 3.A).

---

### 5. Remediation & Hardening Recommendations

To guarantee strict computational monotonicity in Blink's `AudioParamTimeline`:

1. **Double-Precision State Accumulation:**
   Maintain `value` and `multiplier` (for exponential) or `step` (for linear) in `double` (64-bit) precision across the loop and across render quantum boundaries, casting to `float` only at the moment of writing to the output `values` buffer. (Note: Blink partially does this across quanta, but loses precision inside inner vectorized loops).
2. **Monotonic Clamping:**
   Inside the ramp calculation loops, explicitly clamp the generated value against $v_1$ based on the ramp direction before writing to the buffer:
   ```cpp
   // For an upward ramp (v0 < v1):
   current_value = std::min(current_value, v1);
   // For a downward ramp (v0 > v1):
   current_value = std::max(current_value, v1);
   ```
   This completely eliminates the "Endpoint Snap Discontinuity" overshoot caused by recurrence drift.
3. **Direct Interpolation over Recurrence for Linear Ramps:**
   Instead of $v += \Delta v$, enforce $v(t) = \text{fma}(\alpha, v_1 - v_0, v_0)$ using hardware Fused Multiply-Add (`std::fmaf`), which guarantees correct rounding and preserves monotonicity as long as $\alpha$ is monotonic.
